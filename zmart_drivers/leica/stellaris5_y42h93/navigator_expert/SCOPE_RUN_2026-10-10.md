### What was skipped, and why

None of the skips is a problem. They are the validators' deliberate opt-outs:

- **Autofocus** (adapter step) needs `--allow-autofocus`, which `run_ci` does not pass.
- **The objective turret** (each end-to-end route) needs `--allow-objective`, which `run_ci` never passes.
- **Detector gain** (each end-to-end route): the HyD S 2 exposes no writable gain range, so the validator does not touch it.
- **Ten setting readbacks in the log route** were skipped because the write before them was already recorded as unconfirmed (3.2); the validator does not count the same thing twice.

## 3. What to look at

### 3.1 The parity diagnostic: two jobs have no settings in the log

The reader-parity step compares what the API and the LAS X log say. Everything agreed except the
job settings of the two jobs that were **not** selected:

| Reading | API | Log |
|---|---|---|
| settings[Overview] (selected) | 42 fields | 42 fields, log age 1 s, all fields agree |
| settings[AF Job] | yes | **nothing** |
| settings[HiRes] | yes | **nothing** |

This is the behaviour section 11 of `WAITING_PLAN.md` describes: LAS X writes a job's settings to its
log only when they change, so a job that has not been touched has nothing fresh there. The hybrid
route, which is the production one, takes the API's answer and is unaffected. The step is diagnostic
and non-fatal by design, and it stays that way until the open question in that section is decided.

### 3.2 The log-only route is slow and leaves settings unconfirmed

In the log-only route every settings change was sent and applied (the api and hybrid routes read the
same values back fine), but the log never showed the new value within the four windows of three
seconds, so each write came back `success + UNCONFIRMED` after 12 s. This happened for zoom, scan
speed, resonant flip, sequential mode, image format, frame and line accumulation, frame and line
average, and the pinhole, both on the write and on the restore. That is why this route took 325 s
where the other two took about 18 s. Same cause as 3.1, same open question. Nothing hung: the run
finished on its own, which is what the waiting rule was meant to guarantee.

### 3.3 One job switch waited a full 12 s before the command went out

In the adapter step, `set_state` switching from `Overview` to `AF Job` took 12.6 s and passed. The
driver log shows the `SelectJob` command itself was fired at 23:26:54.68 and confirmed from the LAS X
log 0.5 s later, but the step began at 23:26:42.09. The 12 s before the command is exactly one
full waiting budget (`WINDOWS × WINDOW_S`). The switch back to `Overview` showed no such wait, nor
did the job round trips in the three end-to-end routes. `AF Job` is one of the two jobs whose
settings the log does not carry (3.1), so the most likely place is a reading the switch makes before
it fires, such as the objective lookup that decides whether a compensating stage move is needed.
This was not investigated further during the run; it is recorded here as the one thing in the green
run that is worth a look.
